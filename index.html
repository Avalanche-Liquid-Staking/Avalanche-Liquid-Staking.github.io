What is it

What Avalanche staking is

Avalanche staking means locking AVAX to help secure the Primary Network. Most holders do not run a validator themselves; they delegate their AVAX to one, and the validator's node does the work of validating.

Where staking happens

Staking lives on the P-Chain (Platform Chain), which tracks validators and delegations. Your AVAX usually sits on the C-Chain, so it has to be moved to the P-Chain before it can be delegated.

What you choose

You pick a validator, an amount above the network minimum, and a lock period from about two weeks up to one year. The stake stays locked for that whole period and is returned at the end together with any rewards earned. Read the network rules in the Avalanche docs.

Native vs liquid

Native delegation vs liquid staking

There are two common ways to earn staking rewards on AVAX. This interface does the first one: direct delegation on the P-Chain.

Native P-Chain delegation vs liquid staking
Native P-Chain delegationLiquid staking
What you holdYour AVAX, locked on the P-ChainA receipt token from a third-party contract
Lock periodFixed period you choose; no early exitReceipt token can usually be traded or redeemed sooner

Which fits

Native delegation suits holders who are comfortable locking AVAX for a known period and want no extra contract layer. Liquid staking trades that simplicity for flexibility.

How to stake

How to stake AVAX

Move, then delegate

Delegation happens on the P-Chain, so funds on the C-Chain take one extra step first. Each transfer is signed in your wallet.

Step by step

  1. Connect your wallet and check your AVAX balance on the C-Chain.
  2. Export AVAX from the C-Chain and import it to the P-Chain; both steps cost a small fee in AVAX.
  3. Choose a validator and an amount at or above the minimum delegation.
  4. Choose a lock period, from about two weeks to one year, and review the summary.
  5. Sign the delegation. Your stake and rewards are returned when the period ends.

Keep a little unstaked AVAX for fees, since they are paid in AVAX.

Fees

What Avalanche staking costs

Network fees

Moving AVAX between chains uses an export and an import transaction, each with a small fee paid in AVAX. Submitting the delegation itself also carries a network fee.

Validator delegation fee

Each validator sets a delegation fee, a share of your rewards it keeps for running the node. It is shown for each validator, so compare it before you choose.

Rewards depend on uptime

Rewards are paid only if the validator you picked meets the network's uptime requirement during your period. A validator that is often offline can leave you with reduced or no rewards, though your principal is still returned.

Security

Staking safely

Non-custodial by design

You connect your own wallet and sign every transfer and delegation yourself. The interface builds the transactions; it never holds your funds or keys.

Principal is not slashed

Avalanche does not slash delegated principal. If a validator misbehaves or goes offline, the usual cost is missed rewards, not lost stake.

Before you sign

  • Remember the stake is locked for the full period and cannot be withdrawn early.
  • Check validator uptime and delegation fee before choosing.
  • Confirm the amount, the chain and the end date in your wallet prompt.
  • Use a wallet you control and keep its recovery phrase offline.
FAQ

Avalanche Staking FAQ

How does Avalanche staking work?

You delegate AVAX to a validator on the Primary Network P-Chain for a lock period you choose. The validator secures the network, and when the period ends your stake is returned together with any rewards earned.

Do I need to move AVAX to the P-Chain first?

Yes. AVAX usually sits on the C-Chain, and delegation happens on the P-Chain. You export from the C-Chain and import to the P-Chain, each step costing a small fee in AVAX.

How long is my AVAX locked?

For the whole period you choose, which ranges from about two weeks to one year. There is no early withdrawal, so pick a period you can commit to.

Can I lose my AVAX when staking on Avalanche?

Avalanche does not slash delegated principal, so a validator's misbehavior does not reduce your stake. You can still lose out on rewards if the validator has poor uptime, and the usual wallet and phishing risks apply.

What do validators charge for delegation?

Each validator sets its own delegation fee, a share of the rewards it keeps. Rewards also depend on the validator meeting the network's uptime requirement during your period.

Is this interface custodial?

No. You connect your own wallet and sign each transaction, and the interface never holds your funds or keys. It is an independent tool, not an official Avalanche site.
