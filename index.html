What is Avalanche Liquid Staking?

BENQI is the Avalanche DeFi protocol for staking AVAX into sAVAX, a transferable receipt token that represents a staked position. The BENQI liquid-staking overview describes staking from C-Chain and using sAVAX across DeFi.

Avalanche liquid staking with sAVAX

Avalanche liquid staking usually means staking AVAX through a protocol and receiving a liquid receipt token such as sAVAX that represents the staked position. Instead of locking AVAX directly in a validator delegation position, users receive sAVAX and can hold it, swap it, or use it in supported DeFi apps. The benefit is liquidity while earning staking rewards; the trade-off is smart-contract risk, liquidity risk, and possible price deviation between AVAX and sAVAX. The DefiLlama protocol page for BENQI gives a market-data view of the protocol, and the CoinMarketCap listing for the receipt token is another source for token identification.

sAVAX APY and Avalanche staking rewards

sAVAX APY is variable and should be treated as a live protocol metric, not a fixed yield. The displayed rate changes with network staking rewards, validator performance, protocol fees, and token supply; the Avalanche Builder Hub staking guide explains the base reward mechanics and validator responsiveness. If sAVAX is then used in lending or LP positions, the combined return and risk profile changes, so separate base staking rewards from any extra DeFi incentives without treating APY as a guarantee.

How to unstake or redeem sAVAX

Unstaking sAVAX can be done through the liquid staking protocol's redemption flow or by swapping sAVAX back to AVAX on supported markets. Redemption follows the protocol's rules and may involve waiting periods or available liquidity; the BENQI staking and claiming guide documents the app's cooldown and redemption flow. Swapping can be faster, but the rate depends on DEX liquidity and may include slippage. Before choosing an exit method, compare the received AVAX amount, expected time, price impact, and any app-specific limits.

Avalanche Liquid Staking FAQ

What is sAVAX?

sAVAX is a liquid staking receipt token for staked AVAX, most commonly associated with BENQI Liquid Staking on Avalanche. The CoinGecko BENQI Liquid Staked AVAX page lists the token context, while holders should still evaluate liquidity and demand before relying on a market exit.

Is sAVAX always worth one AVAX?

No. sAVAX is a staking receipt token, and its exchange relationship with AVAX can move with redemption mechanics, accrued rewards, market liquidity, and DEX pricing. Investopedia's stETH overview explains a comparable liquid-staking-token price-deviation risk.

Where can I use sAVAX in DeFi?

sAVAX integrations vary across Avalanche lending markets, DEX liquidity pools, collateral use, and yield strategies. The Aave protocol risk documentation is a useful reference for liquidation, oracle, and collateral-risk concepts, but users should still verify the exact token contract and risk rules in any destination market.

What APY should I expect from Avalanche liquid staking?

Avalanche liquid staking APY is not fixed. The Binance Academy liquid staking yield notes explain that liquid staking returns are tied to staking mechanics while app-level APY can also reflect protocol fees and market conditions. Treat displayed APY as a live estimate, not a guaranteed return.

Do I need AVAX to mint sAVAX?

Yes. Users deposit AVAX to mint or receive sAVAX, and they also need AVAX on Avalanche C-Chain to pay transaction fees. The ethereum.org ERC-20 token guide explains token balances and approvals, so keep enough AVAX after staking for approvals, swaps, or redemption.

What are the main risks of Avalanche liquid staking?

The main risks are smart-contract bugs, validator performance, protocol governance changes, market liquidity, token price-deviation risk, and the added risk of any DeFi protocol where sAVAX is deposited. The arXiv SoK on liquid staking tokens discusses how liquid staking adds protocol-design and market-risk layers, so verify official contracts and avoid unofficial lookalike staking pages.
